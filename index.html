What this means

Facebook's explosive 445% growth in views is the standout story of the period. The doubling of unique viewers to 401,400 people — up 467% — shows Wildwood's content is reaching an increasingly wide and diverse audience on the platform. This level of growth is not accidental; it reflects a content strategy that is resonating strongly with Facebook's algorithm and the target demographic.

Facebook's Role in Building Retail Demand

The scale of Facebook's view growth correlates directly with what we're seeing in retail store metrics — a 43% increase in average basket size. As Wildwood content reached over 400,000 unique people on Facebook during this period, those viewers are more likely to recognise and trust the brand at point of sale. The awareness campaign investment is converting into in-store revenue.

Reading the split

While total views dipped 17.4%, Instagram's reach jumped 54.2% — meaning fewer repeat views, but content reaching significantly more unique people. This is a healthier growth signal than the inverse: Wildwood is expanding its audience rather than repeatedly showing content to the same people.

Positive Signal
Reach Growth Outpaces View Drop
171K unique accounts reached — up 54.2% — means Wildwood is being discovered by new audiences consistently, which is the primary goal of the current growth phase.
Strategy
Intentional Shift: Reach Over Impressions
The view dip reflects a deliberate campaign strategy — ads were optimised to maximise reach rather than impressions. This means the priority was putting Wildwood in front of as many new unique people as possible, rather than serving content repeatedly to the same audience. A 54.2% rise in unique reach confirms that strategy worked exactly as intended.
Opportunity
Reel Format to Drive Views Recovery
Reels consistently outperform static and carousel content for raw view counts on Instagram. Increasing Reel frequency is the primary lever to recover views while maintaining reach growth.

The Connection Between Awareness and Retail Basket Size

The 43% jump in average order value at retail stores is not coincidental — it maps directly to the period of sustained awareness campaign investment. When consumers see a brand repeatedly across social media before visiting a stockist, they arrive at the shelf already decided. They buy more because they already trust the product. This "pre-sold customer" effect is one of the hardest marketing outcomes to attribute, but the numbers make the case clearly: Wildwood customers are buying at nearly R3,200 per transaction versus R2,200 a year ago. That R963-per-transaction uplift, multiplied across every retail sale, represents a significant and growing revenue impact that will compound as brand awareness continues to build.

Fetchwave Commentary

What the Data Is Telling Us

Facebook Is the Growth Engine Right Now

A 445% increase in Facebook views and 467% in unique viewers is exceptional. Facebook is the platform where Wildwood content is compounding — each post reaching a wider audience than the last. The algorithm is rewarding the content, which means consistency here should be the top priority for the next phase.

Instagram Reach Growth Is the Right Signal

Don't be misled by the 17.4% view dip on Instagram. Reach grew 54.2% — meaning Wildwood found 54% more new people than the same period prior. This is the healthier metric at this stage of brand growth. The objective is audience expansion, not repeat views from the same followers.

2.1 Million Brand Touchpoints

Combining social views across Facebook and Instagram with paid ad impressions, Wildwood achieved over 2.1 million content touchpoints in 6 months. The 1.17M social views reflect a mix of paid and content-driven reach — together with 940K ad impressions, this represents a significant and consistent brand presence throughout the period.

New Customers Are Buying Big

A R1,774 average first purchase is a strong indicator of product confidence. These aren't trial buyers picking up a single unit — they're committing significant spend from the first interaction. This is the profile of a customer who is likely to return, and who arrived at the brand well-informed by prior social exposure.

The Retail AOV Story Should Be the Lead

The 43% increase in average order value at retail stores is arguably the single most commercially significant data point in this report. It directly links brand awareness investment to in-store revenue growth. Every stakeholder — from retailers to investors — should understand this number and what it represents.

The Flywheel Is Starting to Turn

The combination of strong social view growth, healthy paid impression volume, growing new customer acquisition, and rising in-store basket sizes all point to the same thing: Wildwood's brand investment is beginning to compound. The next 6 months, with continued consistency, should accelerate all of these metrics significantly.
